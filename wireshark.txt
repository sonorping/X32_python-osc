By default, Wireshark does not automatically bind UDP port 10023 to its built-in OSC dissector, which causes X32 traffic to appear as generic "UDP" data rather than parsed OSC bundles and messages.

You can configure Wireshark to decode port 10023 as OSC either dynamically per session or permanently in your global settings.

Method 1: Decode As... (Quickest, Session-Based)
Use this method if you already have a capture running or an open .pcap file:

Right-click any packet sent to or from the X32 on port 10023 in the packet list.

Select Decode As... from the contextual menu.

In the dialog window that appears:

Field: Verify it is set to UDP port.

Value: Verify it displays 10023.

Current: Click the drop-down menu, scroll to or type OSC, and select Open Sound Control.

Click OK (or Save).

The protocol column immediately updates from UDP to OSC, and expanding the packet tree will now break down:

OSC Message / Bundle header

OSC Address: (e.g., /-action/loadscene or /xremote)

OSC Type Tag: (e.g., ,i for integer)

OSC Arguments: (e.g., 2 for Scene 3)

Method 2: Permanent Preferences Mapping (Persists Across Restarts)
To ensure Wireshark decodes port 10023 as OSC every time you launch it:

Open Wireshark settings:

Press Cmd + , (or go to Wireshark > Preferences...).

Expand the Protocols menu in the left sidebar.

Scroll down alphabetically and click on OSC.

Locate the field labeled OSC UDP Ports:

Change it from the default (typically empty or 8000) to include 10023.

If there are other ports listed, separate them with a comma (e.g., 8000, 10023).

Click OK.
